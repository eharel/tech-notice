What it's for

When you're reimaging or updating a room full of machines, two things keep going wrong: the screen sleeps mid-job, and someone wanders up and starts using the computer you're halfway through. This page solves both. Open it on the machine, press F, walk away.

It replaces the old tricks — a 24-hour YouTube video for the sleep problem, a Notepad window reading "DO NOT TOUCH" for the people problem.

Quick start

  1. Open the sign on the machine you're working on.
  2. Press C and fill in your name, extension, and what you're doing. Hit Apply.
  3. Press F for full screen, then get on with the job.

Once you've set it up, bookmark the link the settings panel gives you. That bookmark carries your name and number with it, so next time it's just: open bookmark, press F, walk away.

Keyboard shortcuts

The sign is deliberately bare — it's meant to look like a sign, not an app. Everything is on the keyboard, with one dim ? in the top-right corner as the way in.

KeyWhat it does
COpen the settings panel — name, number, what you're working on
FGo full screen
? or HShow the shortcut card, with a link to this guide
EscClose whichever panel is open; leaves full screen the way it always does
anything elseTriggers the Hands off warning — that's the point

When the page loads, the shortcuts are spelled out in dim text in the top-right corner. After about eight seconds that line fades and collapses to a single faint ?, so a passer-by isn't reading instructions off the sign — but there's still a visible way in for the next person who walks up.

Press ? or click that corner mark and you get a card with the shortcut list and a link to this guide. The card is drawn in the page rather than opened in a tab, so it works even where popups are blocked. The ? is the only thing on the sign you can click without setting off the warning.

The settings panel

C opens it. While it's open the sign behaves normally — typing in the fields won't set off the warning or throw you into full screen.

FieldNotes
Technician name Shows on the sign and in the warning screen.
Extension The (970) 947- part is fixed — type only your last four digits. Non-digits are ignored.
Different number Optional. Anything you type here replaces the extension entirely — a cell, the help desk, whatever. Free-form, no formatting rules.
Work being done Replaces the footer text. Leave it blank and it stays Reimage / setup in progress.

The result reads, in caution yellow under the main message:

Need this machine? Contact Eli Harel · (970) 947-4821

Leave everything blank and the contact line simply doesn't appear — the sign looks exactly as it did before anyone configured it.

Your settings live in the link

There's no account, no database, nothing saved to the machine. Your settings are stored in the page's web address, which is the whole trick: a bookmark carries them, and a bookmark syncs to every machine you sign into.

When you hit Apply, the address bar updates to match. Ctrl+D right then and you're done. The panel also shows the full link with a Copy button, which is the one to use if you want to hand your setup to someone else — or if the address bar didn't update (it won't on some setups, and the Copy box always has the right link).

You can hand-edit these if you like, but the panel is easier and gets the escaping right. Reopening the panel reads them back in — a number in (970) 947-XXXX form returns to the four-digit box, anything else lands in the "different number" field.

What someone sees when they touch it

Any click or keypress that isn't one of the shortcuts flips the screen to a full-bleed red panel that pulses between two shades of red:

HANDS OFF

This computer is mid-service. Typing, clicking, or restarting it
can break the install and start the job over.

Contact Eli Harel · (970) 947-4821

It clears itself after six seconds. Every new poke restarts that six seconds, so someone who keeps mashing keys keeps getting the sign. Your contact details show up here too — this is the moment someone actually wants to know who to call.

Heads up

While the red warning is up it covers everything, including the settings panel's normal entry point. If you walk up to a machine that's mid-warning, just press C — the shortcut clears the warning and opens settings in one go.

Keeping the screen awake

The page asks the browser for a screen wake lock as soon as it loads, and asks again whenever the tab becomes visible after being hidden. That's the same mechanism a video player uses, minus the video.

What that does and doesn't cover:

  • Does: keeps the display from blanking or dimming while the tab is visible.
  • Doesn't: stop a scheduled restart, a sleep policy pushed from management, or the machine suspending because the lid closed.
  • Needs the tab in front. Minimize it or bury it behind other windows and the browser can drop the lock. It re-requests when it comes back, but full screen is the reliable move.
  • Needs a secure page. Loaded over https:// from the link above, you're fine. Some other ways of opening the file won't get a wake lock, and the page won't complain — it just quietly doesn't hold one.
Known gap

If the wake lock is refused, nothing on screen tells you. The sign looks correct and the machine sleeps anyway. Until there's a status indicator, the safe habit is: use the https:// link, and go full screen.

The clock and timer

The footer shows the current time and an elapsed counter. Elapsed counts from when the page loaded, not from when the job started — reload the page and it starts over. Treat it as "how long has this sign been up," which is usually the number you want anyway when you're circling back through a lab.

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause
Machine slept anyway Tab wasn't in front, page wasn't loaded over https://, or a power policy overrode it. Go full screen and use the real link.
Settings vanished You bookmarked the plain address instead of the one from the panel. Press C, Apply, then bookmark.
Address bar didn't change on Apply Harmless — some contexts block it. Use the Copy button in the panel.
Nothing happens on C The page needs focus. Click the page once (you'll get the warning — that's fine, it clears) and try again.
Sign is gone entirely The machine rebooted into the imaging process. Expected — reopen it when it comes back up.

For whoever maintains this

Two files, no build step, no dependencies beyond a Google Fonts link. tech-working.html is the sign; index.html is this guide. Push to main and GitHub Pages serves it.

Adding a setting is four small edits in tech-working.html:

  1. Read the new parameter in readSettings()
  2. Paint it in render()
  3. Add the field to the form
  4. Add a p.set() line in urlFromFields()

Everything from the URL goes onto the page via textContent, never innerHTML — keep it that way, since anyone can hand anyone else a link.
